# Bruce Pocket Lab: A Custom ESP32-S3 Handheld

> Adapting open-source Bruce firmware for a custom ESP32-S3 N8R8 hardware platform: integrating a 1.8-inch ST7735 SPI display, designing a five-button tactile navigation system with long-press escape logic, routing modular RF, NFC, IR, and GPS peripherals, and building a self-contained rechargeable power system.

## ⚡ Project Overview & Engineering Objectives

The **Bruce Pocket Lab** is an ongoing hardware and firmware engineering project to create a portable, standalone embedded multi-tool. While commercial security and diagnostic handhelds exist, they are either closed proprietary devices or built around rigid hardware configurations. The goal of this project is to take the versatile open-source [Bruce firmware](https://github.com/BruceDevices/firmware) ecosystem and adapt it to an accessible, high-performance physical build constructed from discrete breakout modules and custom firmware definitions.

The foundation of the device is the **ESP32-S3 N8R8** microcontroller, which delivers dual-core 32-bit Xtensa processing at 240 MHz alongside 8 MB of on-chip Flash and 8 MB of Octal PSRAM. Around this core, the project interfaces a **1.8-inch ST7735 color TFT display**, a **5-button tactile navigation pad**, an **NRF24L01+ 2.4 GHz radio**, a **PN532 NFC/RFID transceiver**, a **NEO-6M GPS receiver**, a **high-current infrared transmitter/receiver circuit**, and an **18650 Li-ion power subsystem** with active voltage sensing.

### What I Am Building and Why

1. **Hardware-Specific Adaptation:** Rather than relying on generic pre-compiled binaries, this project maintains a dedicated board definition (`boards/esp32-s3-st7735/`) and PlatformIO build pipeline tailored to the exact pinout and timing characteristics of my hardware.
2. **Ergonomic 5-Button Navigation:** Embedded devices without full QWERTY keyboards or touchscreens often struggle with navigation. I designed and tested a 5-button physical input scheme (UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT, SELECT) where a time-delayed hold on the SELECT button triggers Back/Escape (`BTN_ESC`), avoiding the need for a bulky sixth button.
3. **Coexistence of Multiple High-Speed Buses:** Connecting SPI, I²C, UART, analog sensing, and GPIO interrupts on a single microcontroller requires strict bus isolation, shared SPI chip-select coordination, and clean power distribution.
4. **Open Engineering Documentation:** Every step—from initial breadboard bring-up through debugging power drops and verifying GPS NMEA sentence parsing—is captured in the repository with Fritzing diagrams, pinout configurations, and build logs.

*Current Status:* **IN PROGRESS**. The breadboard prototype stage was completed on **27 September 2026** with the successful generation and hardware flashing of the merged 4.29 MB firmware binary. The next phase focuses on transitioning from solderless breadboard to a permanent perfboard assembly and custom 3D-printed enclosure.

## 🔬 Hardware Subsystems

Each subsystem was isolated, verified on breadboard, and documented with dedicated schematics prior to unified system integration.

### 1. Display and Shared SPI Bus

The **1.8-inch ST7735 color TFT** provides a 128×160 pixel canvas for the Bruce menu system, live wireless packet monitors, and telemetry readouts. Because the ESP32-S3 shares its primary SPI peripheral across the display, SD card, and NRF24L01, individual chip-select lines (`GPIO 16` for display, `GPIO 40` for SD, and `GPIO 47` for NRF24) prevent bus collisions.

![ST7735 display connections and shared SPI wiring schematic](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/display.png)

- **Backlight Control:** `GPIO 4` powers the display LED backlight through an internal transistor switch.
- **Shared Bus Lines:** SCK (`GPIO 5`) and MOSI (`GPIO 6`) are shared with the SD socket and NRF24L01 transceiver, while MISO (`GPIO 41`) provides data input from the SD card and radio.
- **Display Driver:** Initialized via custom ST7735 display routines within Bruce's LovyanGFX / TFT_eSPI abstraction layer.
- **Source Link:** [`wiring-diagram/display.png`](https://github.com/V1kraman/Bruce-ESP32-S3-ST7735/blob/main/wiring-diagram/display.png)

### 2. Portable Power Subsystem & Battery Voltage Sensing

Portable operation requires a clean, regulated 5.0 V supply capable of sustaining sudden RF transmission bursts without voltage sag, alongside real-time battery fuel gauge telemetry.

![Portable power circuit schematic showing J5019 module, 18650 cell, switch, and voltage divider](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/power-circuit.png)

```
[ 18650 Li-ion Cell (3.0V - 4.2V) ]
         │
         ├──> SPDT Power Toggle Switch
         │           │
         │           ▼
         │   [ TP4056 Linear Charger & MT3608 Boost Converter (J5019) ]
         │           │
         │           ▼
         │   [ Regulated 5.0V Rail ] ───> ESP32-S3 VIN / 5V Pin
         │
         └──> [ Voltage Divider (2× 100 kΩ Resistors) ]
                     │
                     ├───> 100 nF Noise Filter Capacitor ───> GND
                     │
                     ▼
             [ GPIO 10 (ADC Input) ] (2.1V Max at 4.2V Battery)
```

- **Power Module:** A composite **J5019 module** combines a TP4056 constant-current/constant-voltage lithium-ion charger with an MT3608 inductive boost converter, stepping cell voltage (3.0 V to 4.2 V) up to a solid 5.0 V supply for the ESP32-S3 onboard LDO.
- **Divider Attenuation:** Because an ESP32-S3 analog ADC pin cannot safely exceed 3.3 V (and exhibits non-linear saturation above ~2.6 V with standard attenuation), two precision 100 kΩ resistors divide the raw battery voltage by exactly half ($V_{\text{ADC}} = V_{\text{BAT}} \times 0.5$). When the cell is fully charged at 4.20 V, the ADC sees approximately 2.10 V.
- **Noise Filtering:** A 100 nF ceramic capacitor placed across the divider output to ground shunts high-frequency noise generated by the MT3608 boost converter's 1.2 MHz switching oscillator.
- **Source Link:** [`wiring-diagram/power-circuit.png`](https://github.com/V1kraman/Bruce-ESP32-S3-ST7735/blob/main/wiring-diagram/power-circuit.png)

### 3. Infrared Transmitter and Receiver Circuit

The handheld integrates both infrared transmission and reception capabilities for remote-control cloning and IR signal analysis.

![Infrared receiver and transmitter circuit schematic featuring 2N2222 transistor buffer](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/ir.png)

- **Transmitter Driver:** Microcontroller GPIO pins have a maximum source current of approximately 20–40 mA, which is insufficient for strong IR emitter range. The circuit utilizes a **2N2222 NPN bipolar junction transistor** as an open-collector low-side switch. The ESP32-S3 `GPIO 1` drives the transistor base through a current-limiting resistor, allowing the 940 nm IR LED to draw up to 100 mA pulses directly from the 5 V power rail.
- **Receiver Module:** A standard 38 kHz integrated infrared receiver demodulates ambient carrier signals and passes clean digital pulses directly to `GPIO 2`.
- **Source Link:** [`wiring-diagram/ir.png`](https://github.com/V1kraman/Bruce-ESP32-S3-ST7735/blob/main/wiring-diagram/ir.png)

---

### 4. NRF24L01+ 2.4 GHz RF Transceiver & Regulated Power Adapter

The **NRF24L01+** provides 2.4 GHz ISM band packet transmission, reception, and protocol sniffing.

![NRF24L01 2.4GHz transceiver wiring with dedicated 3.3V adapter module](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/nrf24l01.png)

- **Power Sensitivity:** The NRF24L01 module—especially high-power PA/LNA variants with external antennas—draws instantaneous current spikes exceeding 115 mA during RF transmission bursts. Connecting it directly to the microcontroller's 3.3 V output frequently induces voltage brownouts and microcontroller resets.
- **Adapter Board:** The hardware build utilizes a dedicated socket adapter board equipped with an **AMS1117-3.3 low-dropout regulator** and high-capacity SMD tantalum decoupling capacitors, powered directly from the 5.0 V bus.
- **Bus Connections:** CE (`GPIO 21`), CSN (`GPIO 47`), SCK (`GPIO 5`), MOSI (`GPIO 6`), MISO (`GPIO 41`), and IRQ (`GPIO 35`).
- **Source Link:** [`wiring-diagram/nrf24l01.png`](https://github.com/V1kraman/Bruce-ESP32-S3-ST7735/blob/main/wiring-diagram/nrf24l01.png)

### 5. PN532 NFC / RFID Module

For High-Frequency (13.56 MHz) RFID and NFC card reading, emulation, and cloning, the build interfaces an **NXP PN532 breakout board**.

![PN532 NFC module connections over dedicated I2C bus](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/pn532.png)

- **Interface Selection:** While the PN532 supports SPI, HSU (High-Speed UART), and I²C, it is configured here for **I²C mode** via on-board DIP switches (Channel 1 ON, Channel 2 OFF).
- **Bus Assignments:** Connected to the ESP32-S3 hardware I²C bus on SDA (`GPIO 18`) and SCL (`GPIO 8`).
- **Operating Voltage:** 3.3 V logic and supply levels, preventing any risk of 5 V overvoltage on the ESP32-S3 pins.
- **Source Link:** [`wiring-diagram/pn532.png`](https://github.com/V1kraman/Bruce-ESP32-S3-ST7735/blob/main/wiring-diagram/pn532.png)

### 6. NEO-6M GPS Satellite Navigation

The **u-blox NEO-6M GPS module** provides location telemetry, satellite constellation tracking, speed, altitude, and precision UTC time synchronization.

![NEO-6M GPS module wiring diagram showing hardware UART interconnects](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/neo-6m.png)

- **Hardware Interface:** Communicates asynchronously over a dedicated hardware UART bus:
  - `GPS TX` → `ESP32 GPIO 38` (Hardware RX)
  - `GPS RX` → `ESP32 GPIO 17` (Hardware TX)
- **Baud Rate:** Configured for 9600 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit (8-N-1).
- **Sentence Parsing:** Utilizes the [TinyGPSPlus](https://github.com/mikalhart/TinyGPSPlus) library embedded within the Bruce firmware core to extract `$GPRMC` and `$GPGGA` NMEA sentences.
- **Source Link:** [`wiring-diagram/neo-6m.png`](https://github.com/V1kraman/Bruce-ESP32-S3-ST7735/blob/main/wiring-diagram/neo-6m.png)

### 7. Navigation Controls: 5-Button Tactile Matrix vs. Analog Joystick

The navigation scheme represents an important hardware trade-off in the design. While the project researched both an analog joystick and a discrete 5-button tactile pad, the physical prototype implements the **5-button tactile matrix**.

![Dual-axis analog joystick module wiring reference showing GPIO pin assignments](/images/projects/bruce-pocket-lab/joystick.png)

#### Documented GPIO Overlap & Architectural Choice
In the repository's configuration references (`pinout.txt` and `joystick.png`), the analog joystick pins are documented as:
- `VR-X` → `GPIO 12`
- `VR-Y` → `GPIO 13`
- `SW` → `GPIO 14`

Notice that **GPIO 12, GPIO 13, and GPIO 14 are the exact same pins assigned to the tactile buttons (LEFT: 12, RIGHT: 13, DOWN: 14)**. This is not an error; it represents an intentional design fork:
- **Analog Joystick Option:** Requires continuous dual-channel ADC sampling, deadzone calibration, and suffers from continuous standby power consumption due to potentiometer bias currents.
- **Discrete 5-Button Matrix:** Configured as digital active-low inputs with internal pull-up resistors (`INPUT_PULLUP`). Consumes zero quiescent current when idle, triggers instantaneous edge-detect interrupts, and provides crisp tactile feedback suited for rapid list navigation.

For the physical handheld build, the **5-button tactile configuration** was chosen as the primary input method.

### Milestone 3: Five-Button Navigation & The ~700 ms SELECT Long-Press Engine
- **Period:** Mid-September 2026
- **Objective:** Enable complete, ergonomic navigation through deeply nested firmware menus using only five physical buttons.
- **The Design Problem:** Standard Bruce navigation requires directional movement (Up, Down, Left, Right), confirmation (Select/OK), and cancellation/exit (Back/Escape). With only five physical buttons on the prototype, there was no dedicated hardware Escape button.
- **Software Implementation:** Implemented a software state machine inside the board-specific input handler (`boards/esp32-s3-st7735/interface.cpp`):
  ```
  Button Press Detected (SELECT / GPIO 11 LOW)
            │
            ├── Start Timer: press_start = millis()
            │
            ▼
  Button Released or Polled?
            ├── Released before 700 ms:
            │     Emit BTN_OK (Short Press / Select)
            │
            └── Held >= 700 ms:
                  Emit BTN_ESC (Long Press / Back / Escape)
                  Suppress trailing short-press event on release
  ```
- **Challenges & Debouncing:** A naive long-press implementation would fire a short Select event the moment the button was pressed down, accidentally selecting whatever menu item was highlighted before the user could hold for 700 ms. The input handler had to defer the `BTN_OK` dispatch until button *release*, verifying that total hold duration was under 700 ms.
- **Verification:** Verified across all core menus: main menu browsing, submenu drills, on-screen keyboard text entry, and cancellation back to the home screen.

## 🛠️ Firmware Architecture & PlatformIO Build Pipeline

The firmware utilizes [PlatformIO](https://platformio.org/) to manage dependencies, compiler flags, and board abstractions.

### PlatformIO Environment Configuration

The primary build environment is `[env:esp32-s3-st7735]`, defined in `bruce-firmware/platformio.ini`:

```ini
[platformio]
default_envs = esp32-s3-st7735

[env:esp32-s3-st7735]
extends = env
platform = espressif32
board = esp32-s3-devkitc-1
framework = arduino
board_build.partitions = custom_8Mb.csv
board_build.mcu = esp32s3
board_build.f_cpu = 240000000L

build_flags =
    ${env.build_flags}
    -D ESP32S3_ST7735=1
    -D HAS_5_BUTTONS=1
    -D TFT_WIDTH=128
    -D TFT_HEIGHT=160
    -D TFT_MOSI=6
    -D TFT_SCLK=5
    -D TFT_CS=16
    -D TFT_DC=7
    -D TFT_RST=15
    -D TFT_BL=4
    -D BTN_UP=9
    -D BTN_SELECT=11
    -D BTN_LEFT=12
    -D BTN_RIGHT=13
    -D BTN_DOWN=14
    -D BAT_ADC=10
```

### Partition Scheme (`custom_8Mb.csv`)
Because the ESP32-S3 N8R8 includes 8 MB of Quad-SPI Flash, the build utilizes a custom partition table allocating 4.5 MB for the main app binary, space for a secondary OTA update slot, and an SPIFFS/LittleFS partition for storing configuration files, captured RF codes, and web assets.

### Input Event Dispatch Engine
Inside `boards/esp32-s3-st7735/interface.cpp`, physical button readings are debounced and mapped into Bruce's unified event queue:

```cpp
// Board-specific 5-Button & SELECT Long-Press Engine
void checkButtons() {
    static unsigned long selectPressTime = 0;
    static bool selectHeld = false;

    // Read active-low tactile inputs
    bool upState     = (digitalRead(BTN_UP) == LOW);
    bool downState   = (digitalRead(BTN_DOWN) == LOW);
    bool leftState   = (digitalRead(BTN_LEFT) == LOW);
    bool rightState  = (digitalRead(BTN_RIGHT) == LOW);
    bool selectState = (digitalRead(BTN_SELECT) == LOW);

    if (upState)    dispatchKeyEvent(KEY_UP);
    if (downState)  dispatchKeyEvent(KEY_DOWN);
    if (leftState)  dispatchKeyEvent(KEY_LEFT);
    if (rightState) dispatchKeyEvent(KEY_RIGHT);

    // Long-press handling for SELECT -> ESCAPE
    if (selectState) {
        if (selectPressTime == 0) {
            selectPressTime = millis();
            selectHeld = false;
        } else if (!selectHeld && (millis() - selectPressTime >= 700)) {
            // Held for 700 ms: fire ESCAPE / BACK
            dispatchKeyEvent(KEY_ESC);
            selectHeld = true; // Prevent repeated dispatch
        }
    } else {
        if (selectPressTime > 0) {
            // Released before 700 ms: fire SELECT / OK
            if (!selectHeld && (millis() - selectPressTime < 700)) {
                dispatchKeyEvent(KEY_OK);
            }
            selectPressTime = 0;
            selectHeld = false;
        }
    }
}
```

## 🔍 Technical Troubleshooting & Engineering Log

Engineering embedded systems is defined by diagnosing physical and electrical edge cases. The following log records confirmed issues encountered during development and their verified resolutions:

### Issue 1: GPS Serial Timeout & Stalled Location Updates
- **Problem:** When navigating into the GPS tool, the interface displayed "Waiting for GPS..." indefinitely without updating coordinates.
- **Investigation:** Verified wiring continuity: GPS TX connected to ESP32 RX (`GPIO 38`) and GPS RX to ESP32 TX (`GPIO 17`). Standalone serial monitor confirmed raw `$GPRMC` NMEA sentences were being received from the NEO-6M module at 9600 baud.
- **Root Cause:** The UI drawing routine contained blocking delays that exceeded the hardware serial FIFO buffer, causing NMEA sentences to be dropped before the `TinyGPSPlus` parser could validate complete checksums.
- **Fix / Workaround:** Restructured the serial ingestion routine to poll `Serial1.available()` on every iteration of the non-blocking main loop, continuously feeding the parser before executing display redraws.
- **Verification:** Successfully parsed 8 satellites with a valid 3D fix and HDOP of 1.2 on 21 September 2026 (`prototype_02.jpg`).
- **Lesson Learned:** GPS parsers must be treated as asynchronous streaming consumers; never block or pause the MCU loop when reading NMEA serial data.

---

### Issue 2: Battery Percentage Stuck at 0%
- **Problem:** The status bar battery indicator permanently displayed 0% even with a freshly charged 18650 cell.
- **Investigation:** Measured the voltage at `GPIO 10` with a digital multimeter; it registered a flat 2.50 V regardless of whether the battery was at 3.6 V or 4.1 V.
- **Root Cause:** The high side of the 2× 100 kΩ voltage divider had been wired to the output side of the MT3608 boost converter (which maintains a steady 5.0 V regulated rail), rather than the unboosted battery positive rail (`OUT+`). The divider was measuring the constant boosted rail instead of cell state of charge.
- **Fix:** Rewired the divider input directly to the battery's positive terminal on the J5019 module input.
- **Verification:** At 4.20 V battery voltage, `GPIO 10` measured approximately 2.10 V. The ADC reading decreased smoothly as the cell discharged.
- **Lesson Learned:** Voltage sensing dividers must always monitor the unregulated load side of the battery, upstream of boost regulators.

---

### Issue 3: NRF24L01 Packet Drops and Microcontroller Resets
- **Problem:** Initiating a 2.4 GHz packet scan caused erratic restarts and packet transmission timeouts.
- **Investigation:** Monitored the 3.3 V rail on an oscilloscope during RF transmit bursts. Noticed voltage dips down to ~2.7 V.
- **Root Cause:** The high-power NRF24L01 transceiver draws sudden transient current spikes exceeding 115 mA during PA (Power Amplifier) transmit cycles. The ESP32-S3's internal 3.3 V LDO could not supply this transient current fast enough, triggering a brownout reset.
- **Fix:** Added a dedicated NRF24L01 socket adapter board featuring an onboard AMS1117-3.3 regulator and bulk tantalum decoupling capacitors, powered directly from the 5.0 V bus.
- **Verification:** Continuous 2.4 GHz packet monitoring executed cleanly without system resets or rail droop.
- **Lesson Learned:** Dedicated power regulation and localized decoupling capacitance are non-negotiable for high-frequency RF transceivers.

---

### Issue 4: SELECT Long-Press vs. Short-Press Race Condition
- **Problem:** Holding SELECT to back out of a submenu would immediately select the highlighted item before the long-press duration elapsed.
- **Investigation:** Traced input dispatch callbacks in the interface layer.
- **Root Cause:** The original handler dispatched `KEY_OK` on the *falling edge* (initial button press down) and `KEY_ESC` after a timer expired, causing both events to fire sequentially.
- **Fix:** Converted `KEY_OK` dispatch to occur strictly on the *rising edge* (button release), and only if the total elapsed press time was less than the 700 ms long-press threshold.
- **Verification:** Holding SELECT for ~700 ms cleanly emits Escape without firing Select; short taps cleanly emit Select.
- **Lesson Learned:** Dual-action single-button input schemes must defer short-press actions until button release to avoid premature triggering.

---

### Issue 5: PlatformIO Compiler Warnings & Duplicate Definitions
- **Observation:** During the final merged build, the compiler generated warnings for duplicate `HAS_5_BUTTONS` and `BTN_ALIAS` macros, alongside NimBLE deprecated API warnings.
- **Investigation:** Checked `platformio.ini` build flags against board header files (`boards/esp32-s3-st7735/`).
- **Root Cause:** Build flags defined in `platformio.ini` overlapped with `#define` statements already present in upstream header files.
- **Resolution:** Cleaned up redundant flag declarations. Verified that the generated binary (`Bruce-esp32-s3-st7735.bin`) functioned identically with zero fatal errors.
- **Status:** **Resolved.**
